01

What Is Information Extraction and Why Does It Exist

Language is how humans encode knowledge. But language is designed for humans — it's ambiguous, context-dependent, redundant, and structurally inconsistent. The sentence "Einstein developed the theory of relativity while working in Bern" contains three distinct facts, expressed as a single clause with implicit relationships, temporal context, and a subordinate phrase. A database cannot store that sentence as a queryable fact. A vector index can find it by similarity but cannot reason about its internal structure.

Information Extraction is the field that bridges this gap. It takes unstructured natural language text and converts it into structured, machine-readable representations. OpenIE — Open Information Extraction — is a specific branch with one defining characteristic: it operates without a predefined schema.

Traditional information extraction systems were closed. You defined the relationship types you cared about upfront and the system looked for those specific patterns. OpenIE flips this — it extracts whatever relationships are present in the text, without being told what to look for. The output is a set of triples in the form (subject, predicate, object), and the predicate can be anything the text expresses.

02

The Triple as the Atomic Unit of Knowledge

A triple is the minimal representation of a binary relationship between two entities:

$$\mathcal{T} = (s, p, o) \mid s \in \mathcal{E},\ o \in \mathcal{E},\ p \in \mathcal{R}$$

Where $\mathcal{E}$ is the set of all entities and $\mathcal{R}$ is the set of all predicates. Take this sentence from a machine learning paper: "DistilBERT uses knowledge distillation to compress BERT while retaining 97% of its language understanding capabilities."

A well-functioning OpenIE system should extract:

SubjectPredicateObject
DistilBERTusesknowledge distillation
DistilBERTcompressesBERT
DistilBERTretains97% of language understanding capabilities
knowledge distillationapplied to compressBERT

Each triple is an atomic, independently queryable fact. You can store them separately, merge them with triples from other documents, and traverse the relationships between them — none of which is possible with the original sentence.

03

The Linguistic Pipeline — How Classical OpenIE Works

Before LLMs, OpenIE systems were built on explicit linguistic analysis. Understanding this pipeline matters because it explains both why the problem is hard and why LLM-based extraction is such a significant improvement.

Step 1 — Tokenization and Sentence Splitting

Raw text is first split into sentences, then into tokens. This sounds trivial but isn't. Abbreviations, decimal numbers, and domain-specific notation all create edge cases. "Dr. Smith published in vol. 3, p. 14." contains one sentence, not three — but a naive period-splitter gets it wrong.

Step 2 — Part-of-Speech Tagging

Every token is assigned a grammatical role: noun, verb, adjective, preposition, and so on. "Model" as a noun is an entity. "Model" as a verb is a predicate. The POS tagger resolves this ambiguity using context.

Step 3 — Dependency Parsing

A dependency parser builds a tree structure over the sentence that captures the grammatical relationships between words:

uses
├── subject: DistilBERT (nsubj)
├── object: distillation (dobj)
│   └── compound: knowledge (compound)
└── purpose: compress (advcl)
    └── object: BERT (dobj)

Step 4 — Named Entity Recognition

NER identifies spans of text that refer to real-world entities and classifies them by type. In scientific text, this means recognizing that "DistilBERT" is a Model, "knowledge distillation" is a Method, and "BERT" is another Model. This is where domain adaptation matters enormously — general-purpose NER models trained on news corpora will completely miss "IVF-PQ indexing", "BLEU score", and "multi-head self-attention" as meaningful entities.

Step 5 — Coreference Resolution

One of the hardest problems in NLP. Consider: "The transformer architecture was introduced by Vaswani et al. It replaced recurrent networks entirely." Without coreference resolution, "It" becomes a broken subject. With it, all pronouns resolve to their antecedent and you get clean triples throughout.

Step 6 — Triple Candidate Generation

Using the dependency parse, the system generates candidate triples by identifying subject-verb-object patterns. Some systems use hand-crafted extraction patterns; others use learned classifiers. Hand-crafted systems are more interpretable but brittle. Learned systems generalize better but require large amounts of labeled training data.

Step 7 — Confidence Scoring

Not all extracted triples are equally reliable. Classical systems assign confidence scores based on parse tree depth, NER confidence, predicate clarity, and whether coreference resolution was required. Triples below a threshold $c < \tau$ are dropped before storage.

04

Why LLM-Based Extraction Changes Everything

Classical OpenIE pipelines are a chain of models, each of which can fail and propagate errors downstream. A bad POS tag corrupts the dependency parse. A bad dependency parse corrupts the triple candidates. The error compounds at each step.

LLM-based extraction collapses this entire pipeline into a single step:

Extract all (subject, predicate, object) triples from the following text.
For each triple, assign a confidence score from 0.0 to 1.0.
Normalize entity names to canonical forms.
Output as JSON.

Text: {document_text}

The LLM handles tokenization, parsing, NER, coreference resolution, and triple generation simultaneously. This is what this project uses — TRIPLE_EXTRACTION_METHOD=llm in the config.

Advantage 1 — Implicit Coreference LLMs resolve pronouns and nominal references naturally because they process the entire passage with full context, not sentence by sentence.
Advantage 2 — Domain Knowledge A general-purpose LLM already knows that "DistilBERT" is a model, "knowledge distillation" is a method, and "BLEU" is a metric — without domain-specific NER fine-tuning.
Advantage 3 — Predicate Normalization You can instruct the LLM to normalize predicates to canonical verb forms: "uses", "utilizes", "employs", and "applies" all become USES_METHOD. Classical systems produce whatever verb phrase appears in the text.
Advantage 4 — Implicit Relationships "DistilBERT is a compressed version of BERT" implies DistilBERT DERIVED_FROM BERT even though "derived from" doesn't appear in the text. LLMs can extract these. Classical pattern-matching systems cannot.

05

Entity Disambiguation — The Deduplication Problem

Extraction produces raw entity strings. Raw entity strings are messy. The same real-world entity appears under dozens of surface forms across a corpus:

  • "LLM", "LLMs", "large language model", "Large Language Models", "large language models (LLMs)"
  • "BERT", "Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers", "bert-base"
  • "GPT-3", "GPT3", "the GPT-3 model", "OpenAI's GPT-3"

If each surface form becomes a separate node, the graph fragments. You end up with 6 disconnected nodes for what should be a single concept, and none of them accumulate enough edges to be useful for traversal.

This project uses fuzzy string matching with a configurable threshold (ENTITY_MERGE_THRESHOLD=0.92). Before inserting a new entity, the system checks whether a sufficiently similar entity already exists. If the similarity exceeds the threshold, the new mention is merged into the existing node.

The Threshold Tradeoff Set it too high (0.99) and you get fragmentation — "BERT" and "bert-base" become separate nodes. Set it too low (0.70) and you get false merges — "GPT-2" and "GPT-3" get collapsed into the same node, which corrupts the graph.

06

Predicate Normalization and High-Signal Verbs

Raw predicates extracted from scientific text are extremely diverse. A single semantic relationship — "this method improves this metric" — might be expressed as "improves", "enhances", "boosts", "increases", "achieves better results on", "outperforms baselines on". For graph traversal to work, these need to be normalized to a consistent predicate vocabulary.

This project defines a set of high-signal predicates with associated traversal weights:

HIGH_SIGNAL_PREDICATES = {
    "replaces_component_with":        1.0,
    "introduces_training_objective":  0.95,
    "ameliorates_limitation_of":      0.90,
    "benchmarked_superior_to":        0.85,
    "proposes":                       0.80,
    "uses_dataset":                   0.60,
    "achieves_score_on":              0.60,
    "related_to":                     0.30,
}

The weights reflect semantic specificity. A replaces_component_with relationship is highly informative. A related_to relationship tells you almost nothing beyond co-occurrence. During graph traversal, high-weight predicates are prioritized so the retrieval system follows the most semantically meaningful paths first.

07

From Triples to Graph — The Idempotent Merge

Once triples are extracted, normalized, and scored, they need to be inserted into the knowledge graph without creating duplicates. This is handled by idempotent Cypher MERGE operations:

UNWIND $triples AS triple
MERGE (s:Entity {name: triple.subject})
  ON CREATE SET s.type = triple.subject_type, s.mention_count = 1
  ON MATCH SET s.mention_count = s.mention_count + 1

MERGE (o:Entity {name: triple.object})
  ON CREATE SET o.type = triple.object_type, o.mention_count = 1
  ON MATCH SET o.mention_count = o.mention_count + 1

MERGE (s)-[r:RELATION {type: triple.predicate}]->(o)
  ON CREATE SET r.confidence = triple.confidence, r.sources = [triple.source_id]
  ON MATCH SET r.sources = r.sources + triple.source_id
MERGE vs INSERT MERGE says: find this pattern if it exists, create it if it doesn't. A new entity gets mention_count = 1. An existing entity gets its count incremented. A new relationship gets its confidence and source stored. An existing relationship gets the new source appended. The graph evolves continuously — not a snapshot, a living structure.

What Good Extraction Looks Like vs What Bad Extraction Looks Like

Good extraction from "BERT uses a masked language modeling objective and is pre-trained on BooksCorpus and English Wikipedia":

(BERT, pre-trained on, BooksCorpus)0.92

Bad extraction from the same sentence:

TripleProblem
(BERT, uses a masked language modeling objective and is pre-trained on, BooksCorpus and English Wikipedia)predicate swallowed the object; conjunction should be two triples
(it, uses, objective)pronoun not resolved; predicate truncated; object too vague
Bad Triples Actively Corrupt the Graph A node called "it" with high mention_count because pronouns weren't resolved is noise that pollutes every query touching those edges. This is why confidence threshold filtering exists, and why the extraction step is the most critical component in the entire pipeline.

09

The Bigger Picture

Triple extraction is the point where unstructured language becomes structured knowledge. Everything downstream — graph traversal, multi-hop reasoning, provenance tracking, entity-level summarization — depends entirely on the quality of what happens at this step.

Why LLM Extraction Earns Its Place Scientific text is dense, technical, and full of implicit relationships that classical pattern-matching systems miss entirely. An LLM that already understands the domain can extract (DistilBERT, DERIVED_FROM, BERT) and (DistilBERT, OPTIMIZED_FOR, inference efficiency) from a sentence that contains neither "derived from" nor "optimized for" as explicit verb phrases. That gap between what's written and what's meant is where classical extraction fails.
